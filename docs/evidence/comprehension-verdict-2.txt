LIMITS OF THIS RUN (expected, not blockers)

- Amber banner: the scanners are made up and the results are not real. All judges and
  both controls mention it.
- "Sample fault" titles, "a long plain-English title to test wrapping", "Breaks Sample
  source 0.0" and the names Scanner A/B/C.
- A firm headline finding sits over data the banner says is not real.
- "Try the scanners yourself" sits beside "these scanners are made up" (Judges 1 and 3,
  and both controls). This is the closest call in the run, because it is a real tension
  between two parts of the screen. I treated it as a limit because it exists only through
  the banner's wording. It must be checked again on real data, and the lead may want to
  rule on it separately.
- Comprehension by a non-technical reader was not shown. All three judges answer in
  technical terms, so the key's "should also make sense to a non-technical person" is untested.
- Key mismatch: the key names the button "Try it on your own code" and the screen says
  "Try the scanners yourself". Judges reported what the screen says. No grade was affected.


POLISH LIST

1. The warning-count project has three descriptions: "standard test project" in the
   headline, "TerraGoat, a public practice project full of deliberate flaws" in the panel,
   and "the test project" in the column header. Nothing on screen supports "standard".
   The headline joins the 300 and the 4 of 9, which come from different tests, and only
   the panel shows this. (Judges 1 and 2, negative control)
2. Totals are out of 9, 10 and 8, so Scanner C has the fewest misses partly because it
   finished the fewest. (Judges 1, 2 and 3, mutation control)
3. The grid breaks off at row 5 of 10, with that row clipped. Only 2 of Scanner A's 4
   misses are visible, so the headline can't be fully checked without scrolling.
   (Judges 1, 2 and 3, mutation control)
4. Scanner B's header has no "Couldn't finish" line or "it finished" wording, unlike A and
   C. (Judges 2 and 3, mutation control)
5. The "don't predict" caveat is needed, but it reads as undercutting the general claim
   "Scanners miss different things". (Judges 1 and 3, negative control)
6. The hatching behind the "Couldn't finish" label makes it harder to read. Confirmed in
   a.png, though it is still legible. (Judge 2, negative control)
7. The banner and the "Missed" badges share one amber, and "Caught" is a dim navy that
   fades into the background. (Judge 3)


STRONGEST OBJECTIONS FROM QUESTION 6, COMBINED

1. Small, self-chosen test (Judges 1, 2 and 3): only 10 problems, picked by the authors,
   and the page itself says results don't predict real-world performance.
2. Weak measure (Judges 1 and 3; Judge 2 raises the unfinished runs and noise): any new
   warning counts as "Caught", without showing it relates to the hidden problem. Unfinished
   runs are left out of the score, and false positives and warning usefulness aren't measured.
3. Narrow scope (Judges 1, 2 and 3): only code scanning of Terraform, "not complete
   compliance products", and nothing on runtime risk or fit with their own setup.

Also raised by all three, but a limit of this run: the data is not real and the scanners
are anonymous, so nothing can be acted on. Real results remove the anonymity objection;
objections 1 to 3 would remain.


MOST COMMON MISUNDERSTANDING

None. All three judges read the product and the conclusion correctly and chose B.
The nearest thing is friction, not misreading: the 300 and the 4 of 9 come from different
tests while the headline reads as one result (Judges 1 and 2, negative control). Each of
them noticed this.
