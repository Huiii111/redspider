MOST COMMON MISUNDERSTANDING

No judge misunderstood what the product is. The most common drift was in the takeaway.

All three judges framed the point around warning volume: "more warnings doesn't mean better coverage" (Judges 1 and 2) and "many warnings or a clean scan doesn't prove good coverage" (Judge 3). The key's reason is that scanners miss different things, so a clean scan proves little.

No judge stated that reason outright, though all three cited the "caught 2 of those 4" evidence and Judges 1 and 2 quoted or restated the clean-scan line. The headline's "most warnings" framing pulls readers towards warning volume, and that is the one part of the headline with no visible basis.

A secondary risk is reading "Missed N of 10" as a ranking of scanners (see disagreements and polish item 1).
