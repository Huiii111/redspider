## a.png

**1. What is this? Who would use it, and in what situation?**

It's a results page for "BlindSpots", a benchmark of security scanners for cloud setup code (Terraform). The authors hide 10 known problems, run 3 scanners, and show which problems each one misses.

A security or platform engineer might use it when choosing a scanner, or when checking whether a clean scan can be trusted.

**2. What is the single most important thing it tells you? Why is that the case, according to the screen?**

More warnings doesn't mean better coverage: the scanner with the most warnings (300) still missed 4 of the 9 problems it finished.

The screen's reasoning is that scanners miss different things, as at least one other scanner caught 2 of those 4. So a clean scan "only tells you something if you know what it would have missed."

The yellow banner says all of this is sample data, so this shows what the page would claim, not a real finding.

**3. What would you do next, and where on the screen would you click?**

I'd click the "Missed, and no scanner caught it 2" filter, in the row of pills just above the table, right of centre. Those are the gaps that switching or adding one of these scanners wouldn't fix. I'd then open one of those rows to see the code.

The obvious alternative is the red "Try the scanners yourself" button, but I'd want to understand the gaps first.

**4. What looks broken, empty, unfinished or contradictory?**

- **Placeholder content:** rows are titled "Sample fault two" and so on, one is literally "a long plain-English title to test wrapping", and each says "Breaks Sample source 0.0".
- **Made-up data with a firm conclusion:** the banner says the scanners and results aren't real, yet the headline states a finding and the button invites you to try those scanners.
- **Hedged headline:** the line under the hero says the results don't predict how often a scanner catches problems in real projects, which undercuts the large claim above it.
- **Hard-to-compare totals:** the scanners are scored out of different numbers (4 of 9, 5 of 10, 3 of 8). Scanner C has the fewest misses but also the most unfinished runs.
- **Inconsistent column header:** Scanner B has no "Couldn't finish" line, while A and C do.
- **Colour clash:** the warning banner and the "Missed" badges share the same yellow, while "Caught" is a dim navy that fades into the background.
- **Cut-off table:** the table runs off the bottom of the screen at row 5 of 10.

**5. What existing product does this most resemble?**

It most resembles a browser compatibility matrix like caniuse or wpt.fyi, with test cases as rows, the products being compared as columns, and a pass/fail cell at each intersection. The dark styling looks like a typical developer tool.

**6. Give the three most likely reasons a security lead would dismiss this.**

1. **Small, self-chosen test:** there are only 10 problems, picked by the authors, who admit the results don't predict real-world performance.
2. **Nothing to act on:** the data is fake and the scanners are anonymous, so it can't inform a decision yet.
3. **Weak and narrow measure:** any new warning counts as "Caught", even if it may not relate to the hidden problem. It doesn't measure false positives or whether warnings are useful, and it only covers code scanners, not complete compliance products.
